Appliance Energy Consumption

Understanding how much energy your appliances use — and how to save.

In Australia, household appliances account for a significant portion of total energy consumption. From fridges to televisions, every device contributes to your power bill and environmental footprint.

This site provides insights into energy usage patterns, efficiency ratings, and practical tips to help you make smarter choices.

Frequently Asked Questions

In most Australian homes, heating and cooling systems (reverse-cycle air conditioners) consume the most energy, followed by water heaters and refrigerators. Among entertainment devices, televisions and gaming consoles are the biggest contributors.

The Energy Rating Label shows a star rating from 1 to 6 (or 10 for some appliances) and an estimated annual energy consumption in kWh. More stars mean greater efficiency. The label is mandatory for many appliances sold in Australia, including TVs, fridges, dishwashers, and air conditioners.

Simple steps include: turning devices off at the wall when not in use, choosing appliances with higher star ratings, using energy-saving modes, maintaining appliances (e.g., cleaning fridge coils), and considering solar power or time-of-use tariffs to shift usage to off-peak periods.

Smart appliances often offer energy management features, remote monitoring, and usage insights that can help reduce consumption. While they typically cost more upfront, the long-term energy savings and convenience can make them a worthwhile investment — especially for high-use devices like air conditioners and refrigerators.
