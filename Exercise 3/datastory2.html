Data Story 2: The Hardware Drivers of TV Energy Consumption

Investigating how market distribution, physical dimensions, and underlying display technologies dictate household electricity loads.

1. Market Landscape: Dominance of Medium and Large Screens

To understand overall energy trends, we first examine the distribution of registered television models across Australia. The occurrence count across sizing categories reveals that medium-sized televisions form the vast majority of the market, followed closely by large panels and a smaller share of compact small screens.

Figure 1: Model occurrence count categorized across medium, large, and small screen brackets.

2. The Exponential Cost of Scale: Inch-by-Inch Analysis

While medium TVs dominate in sheer volume, physical scale has a compounding effect on electricity usage. Mapping the mean labelled energy consumption (kWh/year) against precise screen sizes in inches shows that power demands grow modestly in smaller brackets before climbing exponentially as screens exceed 35 inches.

Figure 2: Annual energy consumption scaling sharply with physical screen size in inches.

This nonlinear jump highlights why physical sizing is the single most critical decision point for residential energy efficiency.

3. Technology Interaction: LCD vs. LED vs. OLED Across Sizes

Beyond raw dimensions, internal display technologies—standard LCD, backlit LCD (LED), and self-emissive OLED—interact differently with each size tier to shape total energy draws.

Figure 3: Comparative energy impact across LCD, LCD (LED), and OLED panel types by size category.

In large-format setups, LCD (LED) and OLED configurations command the highest aggregate energy volumes, reflecting the power required to illuminate or drive premium, high-resolution large displays.

Key Takeaways & Recommendations

Smart Sizing: Limiting screen upgrades to medium formats where possible prevents runaway household electricity costs.
