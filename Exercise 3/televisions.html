Televisions & Energy Consumption

How much power does your TV use? Compare models, understand ratings, and save.

Understanding TV Energy Use

Modern televisions vary widely in energy consumption. Screen size, display technology (LED, OLED, QLED), and usage habits all play a role. The average 55-inch LED TV consumes about 80–100 watts per hour, while larger or older models can use significantly more.

In Australia, televisions are required to display an Energy Rating Label. This label shows the estimated annual energy consumption (kWh/year) and a star rating (up to 6 stars). Higher stars mean lower running costs.

Tips to Reduce TV Energy Consumption

  • Turn off: Switch the TV off at the wall when not in use (standby power can add up).
  • Dim the screen: Reduce brightness and enable energy-saving or eco modes.
  • Choose wisely: When buying, compare Energy Rating Labels — a 5-star TV can save you $50+ per year.
  • Size matters: A smaller TV generally uses less energy. Choose the right size for your room.
  • Update firmware: Manufacturers sometimes improve energy efficiency with software updates.
